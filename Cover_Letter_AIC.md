## Summary of the Study

This retrospective cohort study utilized the MIMIC-IV v3.1 database (546,028 total admissions, 2008–2022) to evaluate DSI (HR/DBP) as an independent, zero-cost bedside predictor of in-hospital mortality in critically ill patients with acute abdomen, with external validation in the eICU-CRD. Among 5,728 complete-case ICU stays (in-hospital mortality 19.9%), DSI maintained independent prognostic value (OR=2.18, 95% CI 1.79–2.65, P=7.59×10⁻¹⁵) after adjustment for SOFA and established ICU covariates. The primary extended baseline model excluded abdominal surgery to avoid survivorship bias (surgery "during hospitalization" includes procedures after the outcome; only 5.1% had surgery ≤24h). Adding DSI yielded AUC=0.790 vs baseline 0.785 (ΔAUC=0.005, DeLong P=0.012). We transparently acknowledge that ΔAUC is below conventional clinical relevance thresholds (≥0.02 per Cook 2007, Vickers 2008), and that categorical NRI crosses zero (0.008, 95% CI −0.009 to 0.044), indicating DSI does not reclassify patients across clinically relevant risk strata. DSI's clinical value therefore lies in its independent association (OR=2.18), dramatic quartile mortality gradient (12.1%→32.8%), and zero-cost, immediately available bedside risk stratification complementary to SOFA when laboratory data are pending. External validation in eICU-CRD (N=5,755, 208 hospitals) preserved discrimination (AUC=0.792) and replicated the quartile gradient (12.0%→33.5%), though calibration required local recalibration (intercept shift −3.935). Multiple imputation (N=8,933, AUC=0.822) and 12-scenario sensitivity analyses confirmed robustness.

## Why This Study Fits *Annals of Intensive Care*

1. **Novelty**: This is the first study to systematically evaluate DSI — originally described by Ospina-Tascón et al. in *Annals of Intensive Care* (2020) in the context of septic shock — in the acute abdomen ICU population. DSI's prognostic value in this population has not been previously assessed.

2. **Clinical relevance for intensive care**: Acute abdomen requiring ICU admission carries mortality rates of 15–20%. A zero-cost, immediately calculable bedside tool that complements SOFA for rapid risk stratification is of direct practical value to intensivists.

3. **Methodological rigor**: STROBE and TRIPOD+AI-compliant reporting; primary model excludes surgery to avoid survivorship bias; transparent acknowledgment that ΔAUC=0.005 is below clinical relevance thresholds; bootstrap validation, DeLong testing, VIF diagnostics, NRI/IDI, RCS, competing risks, multiple imputation, 12 sensitivity analyses; excluded patient characteristics reported; un-recalibrated and recalibrated eICU metrics both reported; logistic recalibration intercept shift disclosed.

4. **Timeliness**: DSI has emerged as a research hotspot in 2024–2025, with multiple studies published in critical care journals. Our study extends this evidence to a previously unexamined patient population, filling a clear gap in the literature.
